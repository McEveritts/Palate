# Deep RL Alignment Stack: Optimizing Mid-Sized LLMs for Structured Culinary Reasoning, Multi-Turn Consistency, and Long Context (GRPO, PPO, DPO, RLAIF)

## Executive Summary
This report presents a deep technical and literature-based research stack on Reinforcement Learning (RL) alignment methods, specifically **Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)**, **Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO)**, **Direct Preference Optimization (DPO)**, and **Reinforcement Learning from AI Feedback (RLAIF)**. It evaluates their performance when scaling to mid-sized Large Language Models (LLMs) in the **30B–70B parameter range** (such as Gemma 4 31B, Qwen 2.5 32B, and Llama 3 70B). 

Our primary focus is optimizing these models for **multi-turn conversational consistency**, **structured reasoning block extraction** (using `<thought>...</thought>` tags), and **preserving context-window capabilities** (up to 128k tokens). To frame this within the **Palate (formerly Umami) Architecture**, we illustrate how these techniques are deployed to train a local, high-precision culinary model (e.g., Gemma 4 31B) to master complex food science reasoning, zero-waste kitchen planning, and long-horizon multi-turn recipes without experiencing style collapse, alignment tax, or retrieval degradation.

---

## 1. Introduction and Architectural Context
Mid-sized LLMs (30B–70B parameters) represent the "sweet spot" for specialized, domain-specific deployments. They are large enough to possess sophisticated emergent reasoning capabilities (such as understanding chemical changes during cooking or planning nutritional schedules) while remaining small enough to run efficiently on accessible hardware. 

However, aligning a 30B–70B model to serve as a precise digital sous-chef (**Sage (🌿)**) presents three unique training challenges:
1. **Formatting Integrity:** Forcing the model to output its chain-of-thought within structured XML tags (`<thought>...</thought>`) before outputting its final response, ensuring a clear separation between raw food science reasoning and the clean, presented answer.
2. **Long-Context Robustness:** Ensuring that the model can reference a massive 128k token culinary Markdown vault (`vault/mains/`, `vault/sides/`) using RAG, without losing retrieval performance (needle-in-a-haystack tasks) or suffering from attention degradation over long recipes.
3. **Conversational Coherence:** Maintaining semantic consistency, persona adherence, and active timers/states over 20+ turns of active kitchen guidance.

To solve this, modern reinforcement learning pipelines combine offline preference optimization (DPO) and online relative policy optimization (GRPO/PPO) using feedback loops generated by proprietary models (RLAIF).

## 2. Mathematical Foundations of LLM Alignment Methods

### 2.1 Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO)
Historically, PPO has been the standard algorithm for Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF). In LLM alignment, it is formulated as an Actor-Critic architecture.

#### Objective Function:
$$L_{PPO}(\theta) = \hat{\mathbb{E}}_{t} \left[ \min \left( r_t(\theta)\hat{A}_t, \, \text{clip}(r_t(\theta), 1-\epsilon, 1+\epsilon)\hat{A}_t \right) \right] - \beta D_{KL}(\pi_\theta || \pi_{ref})$$

Where:
* **$r_t(\theta) = \frac{\pi_\theta(y_t | x, y_{<t})}{\pi_{\theta_{old}}(y_t | x, y_{<t})}$** is the token-level policy ratio.
* **$\hat{A}_t$** is the token-level advantage, estimated using Generalized Advantage Estimation (GAE) based on a learned value function $V_\phi(x, y_{<t})$.
* **$D_{KL}(\pi_\theta || \pi_{ref})$** is the Kullback-Leibler divergence penalty against a frozen reference model (typically the SFT baseline) to prevent policy drift:
  $$D_{KL}(\pi_\theta(y|x) || \pi_{ref}(y|x)) = \sum_{t=1}^T \log \frac{\pi_\theta(y_t | x, y_{<t})}{\pi_{ref}(y_t | x, y_{<t})}$$

#### Computational Footprint & Bottleneck:
Training a 30B–70B model with PPO requires maintaining **four models** in active memory:
1. **Policy Network $\pi_\theta$** (active, gradients computed).
2. **Critic Network $V_\phi$** (active, gradients computed, usually similar in scale to $\pi_\theta$).
3. **Reference Network $\pi_{ref}$** (frozen, forward pass only).
4. **Reward Model $R_{RM}$** (frozen, forward pass only).

This results in a massive memory tax (often requiring 4x the base VRAM of the model size), making standard PPO highly inefficient and unstable for 30B–70B models without extensive tensor and pipeline sharding.

### 2.2 Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)
Introduced in **DeepSeekMath (2024)** and scaled for **DeepSeek-R1 (2025)**, GRPO addresses the PPO memory tax by **eliminating the Critic Network** entirely.

#### Advantage Estimation:
For each prompt $q$, the policy $\pi_{\theta_{old}}$ samples a group of $G$ outputs: $\{o_1, o_2, \dots, o_G\}$. The reward $r_i$ of each completion $o_i$ is evaluated. The advantage $A_i$ is computed relative to the group's rewards:
$$A_i = \frac{r_i - \text{mean}(\{r_1, \dots, r_G\})}{\text{std}(\{r_1, \dots, r_G\}) + \epsilon_0}$$

This group normalization provides a highly stable baseline, identifying which responses performed better than the local average.

#### Objective Function:
$$J_{GRPO}(\theta) = \frac{1}{G} \sum_{i=1}^G \left[ \min \left( \frac{\pi_\theta(o_i|q)}{\pi_{\theta_{old}}(o_i|q)} A_i, \, \text{clip} \left( \frac{\pi_\theta(o_i|q)}{\pi_{\theta_{old}}(o_i|q)}, 1-\epsilon, 1+\epsilon \right) A_i \right) - \beta D_{KL}(\pi_\theta(o_i|q) || \pi_{ref}(o_i|q)) \right]$$

#### Computational Footprint & Bottleneck:
By discarding the Critic network, GRPO saves **40–60% of VRAM** compared to PPO. The reference model $\pi_{ref}$ can also be dynamically offloaded or fused, meaning that only the policy network $\pi_\theta$ is fully active in training. This makes GRPO highly stable and efficient for reasoning models (e.g., training a Qwen 32B model on standard consumer-grade clusters or single node multi-GPU setups).

### 2.3 Direct Preference Optimization (DPO)
DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023) eliminates the online RL loop entirely. It analytically shows that the standard RLHF objective can be solved in closed form, translating policy optimization into a supervised classification problem on preference pairs.

#### Objective Function:
$$L_{DPO}(\theta; \pi_{ref}) = -\mathbb{E}_{(x, y_w, y_l) \sim D} \left[ \log \sigma \left( \beta \log \frac{\pi_\theta(y_w|x)}{\pi_{ref}(y_w|x)} - \beta \log \frac{\pi_\theta(y_l|x)}{\pi_{ref}(y_l|x)} \right) \right]$$

Where:
* **$y_w$** is the preferred (winning) response.
* **$y_l$** is the dispreferred (losing) response.
* **$\sigma$** is the sigmoid function.

#### Core Challenges at 30B–70B:
* **No Online Exploration:** DPO is purely offline. It cannot generate new trajectories and explore alternative reasoning paths. It is highly prone to over-fitting, style hacking (e.g., learning that "longer responses always win"), and dramatic performance collapse if the offline dataset contains noise.
* **KL Contraction:** Standard DPO can excessively squeeze the token distribution, causing the model to lose entropy and repeat text during inference.

---

### 2.4 Reinforcement Learning from AI Feedback (RLAIF)
RLAIF (pioneered by Anthropic's Constitutional AI and Google) decouples preference labeling from human annotations. It employs a teacher model (e.g., GPT-4 or a fine-tuned Llama-3-70B-Instruct) to evaluate generations and assign preferences.

#### Preference Probability Formulation:
Given responses $y_1$ and $y_2$ for prompt $x$, the AI judge evaluates them based on a constitution (e.g., "Which response shows a more precise scientific explanation of the Maillard reaction?"). The logits of the judge's selected option (`(A)` or `(B)`) are extracted:
$$P(y_1 \succ y_2 | x) = \frac{\exp(\text{logit}_J(\text{"(A)"}))}{\exp(\text{logit}_J(\text{"(A)"})) + \exp(\text{logit}_J(\text{"(B)"}))}$$

This soft probability distribution is used directly in a weighted DPO objective:
$$L_{wDPO}(\theta) = -\mathbb{E} \left[ P(y_1 \succ y_2 | x) \log \sigma \left( \beta \log \frac{\pi_\theta(y_1|x)}{\pi_{ref}(y_1|x)} - \beta \log \frac{\pi_\theta(y_2|x)}{\pi_{ref}(y_2|x)} \right) + P(y_2 \succ y_1 | x) \log \sigma \left( \beta \log \frac{\pi_\theta(y_2|x)}{\pi_{ref}(y_2|x)} - \beta \log \frac{\pi_\theta(y_1|x)}{\pi_{ref}(y_1|x)} \right) \right]$$

## 3. Structured Reasoning Block Extraction & Formatting Integrity

To train a mid-sized model (e.g., Gemma 4 31B) to act as a **professional digital sous-chef (Sage)**, it must generate a structured reasoning block wrapped in `<thought>...</thought>` tags before outputting the culinary instructions. This separates complex food science calculations (e.g., converting baker's percentages, scaling ingredients, or planning thermodynamic heat transfers) from the user-facing recipe.

### 3.1 Reward Design for Formatting & Accuracy
DeepSeek-R1-style training uses **Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR)**. We implement three rule-based rewards:

1. **Accuracy Reward ($R_{acc}$):** Evaluates if the final culinary output contains correct values (e.g., proper scaling of sourdough hydration, exact metric measurements in grams/ml).
2. **Syntactic Formatting Reward ($R_{form}$):** Enforces XML structure:
   * **Full Match (+1.0):** Output contains exactly one `<thought>` and one `</thought>` tag, and the thought block is entirely closed before the main response begins.
   * **Incorrect Ordering (-1.0):** `</thought>` occurs before `<thought>` or final answer spills into the thought block.
   * **Missing Tags (-2.0):** No thought tags present.
3. **Length and Repetition Penalty ($R_{len}$):** During RL, models naturally discover that generating excessively long, circular reasoning chains increases token-level probabilities (a form of reward hacking). We apply a length penalty if the thought block exceeds 2,000 tokens without increasing correctness, and a repetition penalty based on n-gram frequency to block infinite loops.

```
       [Prompt: "Scale recipe to 75% hydration"]
                           │
                           ▼
              ┌─────────────────────────┐
              │    Model Generation     │
              └────────────┬────────────┘
                           │
             ┌─────────────┴─────────────┐
             ▼                           ▼
      [Extract Block]             [Final Recipe]
    <thought>...</thought>         "Ingredients..."
             │                           │
             ▼                           ▼
    [Format Evaluator]           [Culinary Solver]
  Matches XML syntax? +1.0      Hydration correct? +1.0
             │                           │
             └─────────────┬─────────────┘
                           │
                           ▼
                  [Compute Advantage]
                           │
                           ▼
                  [Update Policy via GRPO]
```

### 3.2 Guarding Against "Format Collapse"
Format collapse occurs when a model loses the capacity to generate the closing `</thought>` tag or outputs empty thoughts. To prevent this during RL training of a 30B-70B model:
* **SFT Bootstrapping (Warming):** The model is first fine-tuned on a high-quality dataset of 5,000–10,000 examples containing perfect CoT thought formatting.
* **Constrained Grammar Decoding:** During RL online sampling, we can enforce a formal grammar (e.g., using SGLang or Outlines) that restricts output to start with `<thought>`, require at least 50 tokens, emit `</thought>`, and then follow with the main markdown content.

## 4. Multi-Turn Conversational Consistency

In active kitchen guidance, a user asks questions step-by-step (e.g., "How is it looking now?", "Is my caramel too dark?"). The model must maintain its elegant, precise professional tone and keep track of active cook times, nutritional structures, and previous steps over 20+ turns.

### 4.1 Trajectory vs. Turn-Level RL
Traditional DPO operates on single-turn preference pairs. In multi-turn dialogue, this leads to semantic drift or model decay at turn 5+. To prevent this, we utilize **Iterative RL** on full dialogue trajectories:

* **Trajectory-level Rewards:** Rather than evaluating each turn in isolation, the AI judge (RLAIF) evaluates the entire conversational flow, penalizing the model if it contradicts itself at Turn 10 compared to Turn 2, or if it forgets the user's active dietary constraints (e.g., diabetic-friendly, zero-waste).
* **Iterative GRPO with Turn-Level Advantage:** For a given conversation history $H_{<k}$ and current user query $u_k$, the policy samples $G$ alternate responses $\{r_{k,1}, \dots, r_{k,G}\}$. The reward is computed based on:
  1. Adherence to the established menu plan.
  2. Tone consistency (professional, sous-chef Sage persona).
  3. No repeating previous instructions.
  The policy is updated online, ensuring that conversational consistency is dynamically reinforced.

## 5. Long-Context Window Preservation (32k - 128k Tokens)

Palate relies on reading recipes, nutritional profiles, and kitchen inventory logs from a local vault. As the context window scales to 128k tokens, aligning the model with standard RL (PPO/DPO) can trigger the **Alignment Tax**, severely degrading the model's fundamental retrieval capacity (e.g., "Needle in a Haystack" accuracy drops from 99% to 70%).

```
Long-Context Retrieval Performance (Needle-in-a-Haystack)
100% |████████████████████████████████  (Pre-trained / SFT Model)
 80% |████████████████████              (Standard DPO/PPO Aligned)
 95% |██████████████████████████████    (Aligned with Short-to-Long KL / LongPO)
     └────────────────────────────────
      0k                              128k Context Length
```

### 5.1 Short-to-Long KL Divergence Constraints
To mitigate long-context degradation, recent frameworks like **LongPO (Chen et al., 2025)** and **SoLoPO (Sun et al., 2025)** introduce a short-to-long KL regularization mechanism. 

Standard KL divergence penalizes drift from $\pi_{ref}$ at the same context length. During long-context training, the model's short-context capabilities decay because long-context gradients dominate. 

#### LongPO short-to-long KL objective:
Instead of measuring KL only on the long sequence, we partition the objective. Let $x_{short}$ be a short version of the long context (e.g., a summarized version or a truncated prompt). We regularize the long-context policy $\pi_\theta(y | x_{long})$ by bounding its divergence against the highly stable short-context reference policy $\pi_{ref}(y | x_{short})$:
$$D_{KL}^{S2L} = \mathbb{E} \left[ D_{KL} \left( \pi_\theta(y | x_{long}) \,||\, \pi_{ref}(y | x_{short}) \right) \right]$$

This anchors the model's generative quality and prevents the "forgetting" of short-context capabilities.

### 5.2 Context Faithfulness Preference (Context-DPO)
To prevent the model from hallucinating or utilizing outdated parametric knowledge over highly specific local recipes, we utilize **Context-DPO (Bi et al., 2025)**. 

We construct DPO preference pairs:
* **Prompt ($x$):** A long context containing a specific recipe with modifications (e.g., "Substitute sugar with erythritol at 0.7x weight") and a query ("How much sweetener do I add?").
* **Winner ($y_w$):** A response that faithfully extracts and calculates from the retrieved context ("Add 70g of erythritol").
* **Loser ($y_l$):** A response that hallucinates or defaults to parametric knowledge ("Add 100g of sugar, as standard").

Training on these targeted context-faithful preference pairs forces the model's attention mechanism to remain anchored to the provided context, even at 128k tokens.

### 5.3 Infrastructure & Distributed Scaling
Training a 30B–70B model with 128k context lengths during RL requires extreme optimization. The **veRL** (Volcano Engine RL) framework by ByteDance resolves this via a **3D-HybridEngine**:
* **Actor-vLLM Integration:** During online sampling (rollout), veRL deploys vLLM as the generation engine. vLLM uses **PagedAttention** and vLLM's tensor-parallel workers to achieve maximum throughput during generation.
* **FSDP Training:** During backpropagation, veRL dynamically reshards the policy parameters using PyTorch Fully Sharded Data Parallel (FSDP), enabling training of 30B–70B models on multi-node clusters.
* **Fused FlashAttention-3:** Saves VRAM by fusing the attention and loss steps, preventing the standard $O(N^2)$ sequence length memory explosion.

## 7. Recommended Alignment Pipeline for Palate

To align a local mid-sized model (e.g., **Gemma 4 31B** or **Qwen 2.5 32B**) to serve as the **Sage (🌿)** professional culinary assistant, we recommend the following step-by-step post-training pipeline:

```
                  [Base Model: Qwen 2.5 32B / Gemma 4 31B]
                                     │
                                     ▼
         ┌───────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
         │     Step 1: Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) Warming      │
         │     - 10k perfect recipe structures                   │
         │     - Consistently uses <thought>...</thought> tags   │
         └───────────────────────────┬───────────────────────────┘
                                     │
                                     ▼
         ┌───────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
         │     Step 2: RLAIF Preference Generation               │
         │     - AI Judge (Llama 3 70B) ranks multi-turn recipes │
         │     - Enforces tone, dietary rules, & faithfulness   │
         └───────────────────────────┬───────────────────────────┘
                                     │
                                     ▼
         ┌───────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
         │     Step 3: Online GRPO Training (veRL / FSDP)        │
         │     - Group Size G = 8                                │
         │     - Rewards: Verifiable math + Format XML syntax    │
         │     - Regularization: Short-to-Long KL Constraint     │
         └───────────────────────────┬───────────────────────────┘
                                     │
                                     ▼
                      [Sage Culinary Reasoning Model]
```

### Step 1: SFT Warming (Bootstrapping)
Fine-tune the base model on a small, ultra-high-quality culinary dataset (10,000 examples) written in Cooklang and Markdown. Ensure every single response begins with `<thought>` followed by a meticulous food science analysis (e.g., starch gelatinization temperature, gluten structure formation, acid-fat balancing), closes with `</thought>`, and presents a metric-first step-by-step recipe.

### Step 2: AI-in-the-Loop preference alignment (RLAIF)
Use a teacher model to generate preference pairs specifically targeted at:
* **Tone consistency:** Sage must remain precise, elegant, and strictly culinary.
* **Context faithfulness (Context-DPO):** Rewarding correct, non-hallucinated extractions from local vault documents.

### Step 3: Online GRPO on Verifiable Outcomes & Formatting
Train the warmed model using the **veRL** framework.
* **Group Size ($G$):** Set $G = 8$ for optimal balance between gradient accuracy and GPU memory.
* **Verifiable Reward Evaluator:** Use Python-based rule checkers to parse the output. Ensure the baking percentages are mathematically correct, unit conversions are precise, and all tags match.
* **Regularization:** Integrate a **Short-to-Long KL constraint** using a reference model running on compressed contexts, ensuring that the aligned model maintains its 128k retrieval capabilities.

This ensures that Palate has a robust, local, state-of-the-art intelligence stack that is CONCEPTUALLY ACCURATE, highly optimized, and runs at the peak of professional culinary technology.
