# 🌿 Connecticut-Style Lobster Rolls

A refined, warm interpretation of a New England classic. Unlike the Maine style, the Connecticut roll eschews mayonnaise in favor of a luxurious, clarified butter poach, highlighting the natural sweetness of the crustacean.

## 🥖 Ingredients

* @grass-fed unsalted butter{4%tbsp}
* @fresh lobster meat{1%lb} (cut into bite-sized chunks)
* @fresh lemon juice{1%tbsp}
* @organic parsley{1%tbsp} (finely chopped)
* @sea salt{1/4%tsp}
* @cracked black pepper{1/8%tsp}
* @whole-grain brioche buns{4%pieces} (split top)

## 👨‍🍳 Preparation

I. **Butter Emulsion:** In a wide skillet over medium-low heat, melt the @grass-fed unsalted butter{4%tbsp} until shimmering but not browned. 
**Technique Note:** Keep the heat low to avoid breaking the emulsion; we want a silky coating, not a fried texture.

II. **Poaching:** Gently fold in the @fresh lobster meat{1%lb}. Toss lightly to coat each piece in butter. Simmer for 3–5 minutes just until warmed through. 
**Crucial Step:** Do not overcook the lobster at this stage, as it is likely already pre-cooked; overheating will result in a rubbery texture.

III. **Finishing:** Stir in the @fresh lemon juice{1%tbsp}, @organic parsley{1%tbsp}, @sea salt{1/4%tsp}, and @cracked black pepper{1/8%tsp}. Remove from heat immediately.

IV. **The Toast:** Lightly brush the insides of the @whole-grain brioche buns{4%pieces} with a small amount of the remaining butter from the pan. Toast in a separate skillet or under a broiler until golden brown.

V. **Assembly:** Spoon the warm lobster generously into the toasted buns. Garnish with an extra pinch of parsley and a lemon wedge.

## 💡 Chef's Additions & Troubleshooting

* **The Maillard Reaction vs. Poaching:** If your buns are too soft, ensure they hit a hot pan (approx. 350°F) to trigger the Maillard reaction quickly. This creates a structural crispness that prevents the butter from soaking through and making the bread soggy.
* **Temperature Control:** If the lobster seems too dry, add a teaspoon of @filtered water{1%tsp} to the butter. This creates a tiny amount of steam that rehydrates the proteins without adding excess fat.
* **Acid Balance:** The @fresh lemon juice{1%tbsp} serves as a chemical counterbalance to the saturated fats of the butter. If the dish tastes "heavy," increase the acidity slightly to brighten the flavor profile.
