# 🌿 Hoppin John

A refined, clean-eating interpretation of the Southern classic. This version replaces processed meats with smoked paprika and aromatic vegetables to achieve a deep, savory profile while maintaining nutritional integrity.

# 🛒 Ingredients

* @olive oil{2%tbsp}
* @yellow onion{1%medium, diced}
* @celery{2%stalks, diced}
* @green bell pepper{1%medium, diced}
* @garlic{3%cloves, minced}
* @dried black-eyed peas{1%cup, soaked overnight and drained}
* @brown rice{1%cup, rinsed}
* @low-sodium vegetable broth{4%cups}
* @smoked paprika{1%tsp}
* @dried thyme{1%tsp}
* @sea salt{1%tsp}
* @black pepper{1/2%tsp}
* @fresh parsley{1/4%cup, chopped}

# 👨‍🍳 Instructions

I. Heat the @olive oil{2%tbsp} in a large pot or Dutch oven over medium heat. Add the @yellow onion{1%medium}, @celery{2%stalks}, and @green bell pepper{1%medium}. Sauté for 5-7 minutes until softened. **Technique Note:** This "Holy Trinity" of vegetables forms the aromatic base; ensure they are translucent but not browned to maintain a clean flavor profile.

II. Stir in the @garlic{3%cloves}, @smoked paprika{1%tsp}, and @dried thyme{1%tsp}. Cook for 60 seconds until fragrant.

III. Add the @dried black-eyed peas{1%cup} and @brown rice{1%cup}, stirring well to coat the grains and legumes in the seasoned oil. **Crucial Step:** Toasting the rice and peas for 2 minutes before adding liquid enhances the nutty flavor and prevents the rice from becoming overly mushy.

IV. Pour in the @low-sodium vegetable broth{4%cups}. Bring the mixture to a gentle boil, then immediately reduce heat to low.

V. Cover with a tight-fitting lid and simmer for 45-50 minutes, or until the liquid is absorbed and both the peas and rice are tender.

VI. Remove from heat and let the pot sit, covered, for 10 minutes. This allows the remaining moisture to redistribute evenly.

VII. Stir in @sea salt{1%tsp}, @black pepper{1/2%tsp}, and garnish with @fresh parsley{1/4%cup} before serving.

# 💡 Chef's Additions & Troubleshooting

* **The Science of Soaking:** Soaking the black-eyed peas is not just for texture; it neutralizes phytic acid, an anti-nutrient that can inhibit the absorption of minerals and cause digestive distress. For a faster prep, perform a "quick soak" by boiling them for 2 minutes and letting them sit for one hour.
* **Starch Management:** Brown rice contains an outer bran layer that requires more hydration than white rice. If the dish appears dry before the peas are tender, add @vegetable broth{1/4%cup} at a time. The 10-minute resting period is essential for "retrogradation," ensuring the grains are fluffy rather than gummy.
* **Flavor Depth:** To replicate the traditional smokiness of ham hock without the nitrates, the smoked paprika provides the essential phenolic compounds. For an added layer of complexity, a teaspoon of apple cider vinegar stirred in at the very end brightens the heavy starches through acid-balancing.
