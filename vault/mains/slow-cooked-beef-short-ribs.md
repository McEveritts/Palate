# 🌿 Slow Cooked Beef Short Ribs

An elegant, nutrient-dense interpretation of a classic comfort dish. By utilizing grass-fed beef and a slow-braising technique, we transform tough connective tissues into a melt-in-your-mouth delicacy.

## 🥕 Ingredients

* @grass-fed beef short ribs{3%lbs}
* @avocado oil{2%tbsp}
* @yellow onion{1%large, diced}
* @carrots{3%medium, sliced}
* @celery stalks{2%medium, sliced}
* @garlic cloves{4%cloves, minced}
* @tomato paste{2%tbsp}
* @grass-fed beef bone broth{2%cups}
* @dry red wine{1%cup}
* @fresh thyme{3%sprigs}
* @fresh rosemary{1%sprig}
* @bay leaves{2%leaves}
* @sea salt{1%tsp}
* @black pepper{1%tsp}

## 🍳 Instructions

I. Pat the @grass-fed beef short ribs{3%lbs} completely dry with paper towels and season generously with @sea salt{1%tsp} and @black pepper{1%tsp}.

II. Heat @avocado oil{2%tbsp} in a heavy-bottomed skillet over medium-high heat. Sear the ribs on all sides until a deep brown crust forms. 
**Technique Note:** This is the Maillard reaction; achieving a dark sear is essential for the depth of flavor in the final sauce.

III. Remove ribs and set aside. In the same pan, add @yellow onion{1%large}, @carrots{3%medium}, and @celery stalks{2%medium}. Sauté for 5–7 minutes until softened.

IV. Stir in @garlic cloves{4%cloves} and @tomato paste{2%tbsp}, cooking for another 2 minutes until the paste turns a deep rust color.

V. Pour in @dry red wine{1%cup} to deglaze the pan, scraping the bottom with a wooden spoon to release the fond. 

VI. Transfer the aromatic mixture and the seared ribs into the slow cooker. Pour over the @grass-fed beef bone broth{2%cups}.

VII. Tuck in the @fresh thyme{3%sprigs}, @fresh rosemary{1%sprig}, and @bay leaves{2%leaves}.

VIII. Cover and cook on **Low for 8 hours** or **High for 4–5 hours**.
**Crucial Step:** Avoid opening the lid during the cooking process to maintain a constant internal temperature and prevent moisture loss.

## 💡 Chef's Additions & Troubleshooting

* **The Science of Collagen:** Short ribs are rich in connective tissue (collagen). Slow cooking at low temperatures allows this collagen to hydrolyze into gelatin, which provides the signature "sticky" mouthfeel and tenderness. If the meat is still tough, it simply needs more time; collagen breakdown is a function of time and temperature.
* **Sauce Reduction:** For a restaurant-grade finish, strain the cooking liquid into a saucepan after cooking. Simmer over medium-high heat for 10 minutes to reduce the volume by one-third. This concentrates the flavors and creates a glossy, velvet-like glaze.
* **Acid Balance:** If the final sauce tastes too "heavy" or fatty, stir in a teaspoon of apple cider vinegar or a squeeze of fresh lemon juice just before serving. The acidity cuts through the richness of the beef fat, brightening the overall flavor profile.
