# 🍑 Ingredients

- @Dry Riesling{0.5%cup}
- @fresh peach puree{1%cup}
- @pure maple syrup{0.25%cup}
- @Dijon mustard{1%tbsp}
- @apple cider vinegar{1%tbsp}
- @ground cinnamon{0.25%tsp}
- @sea salt{0.5%tsp}
- @black pepper{0.25%tsp}

# 👨‍🍳 Instructions

I. In a small saucepan over medium heat, combine the @Dry Riesling{0.5%cup}, @fresh peach puree{1%cup}, and @pure maple syrup{0.25%cup}.

II. Whisk in the @Dijon mustard{1%tbsp}, @apple cider vinegar{1%tbsp}, @ground cinnamon{0.25%tsp}, @sea salt{0.5%tsp}, and @black pepper{0.25%tsp} until the mixture is completely homogenized.

III. Bring the liquid to a gentle simmer. **Technique Note:** Maintain a low simmer rather than a rolling boil to prevent the natural sugars in the peach and maple syrup from scorching or breaking.

IV. Reduce the glaze by approximately half, stirring occasionally, until it reaches a syrupy consistency that coats the back of a spoon. **Crucial Step:** Ensure the reduction is sufficient; if the glaze is too thin, it will run off the ham rather than forming a lacquered crust.

V. Brush the glaze generously over the ham during the final 30 minutes of roasting, applying a fresh layer every 10 minutes to build depth of flavor and color.

# 💡 Chef's Additions & Troubleshooting

*   **The Science of the Glaze:** The combination of the Riesling's acidity and the maple syrup's sucrose facilitates the Maillard reaction and caramelization. This creates a complex flavor profile where the sharpness of the wine cuts through the richness of the ham's fat.
*   **Emulsion Stability:** If the glaze appears to separate, whisk in a teaspoon of cold water or a small knob of grass-fed butter at the very end. This re-emulsifies the fats and acids for a glossier, professional finish.
*   **Flavor Balancing:** If the peach puree is overly tart, increase the @pure maple syrup{0.25%cup} by one tablespoon. Conversely, if the glaze tastes too sweet, add a pinch more @sea salt{0.5%tsp} to brighten the floral notes of the Riesling.
