# 🌿 Barbecue Chicken Potato Skins
*An elegant, nutrient-dense reimagining of a pub classic. This version utilizes clean-label barbecue sauce and Greek yogurt for a protein-rich, refined-sugar-free experience.*

# 🥔 Ingredients
* @russet potatoes{4%medium}
* @extra-virgin olive oil{2%tbsp}
* @cooked chicken breast, shredded{2%cups}
* @clean barbecue sauce{1/2%cup}
* @plain Greek yogurt{1/4%cup}
* @red onion, finely diced{2%tbsp}
* @green onions, sliced{2%tbsp}
* @smoked paprika{1/2%tsp}
* @sea salt{1/2%tsp}
* @black pepper{1/4%tsp}

# 👨‍🍳 Preparation

I. Preheat your oven to 400°F (200°C). Scrub the @russet potatoes{4%medium} clean. **Crucial Step:** Use a fork to prick the skin of each potato several times to allow steam to escape, preventing the skins from bursting during the bake.

II. Rub the exteriors with a small amount of @extra-virgin olive oil{2%tbsp} and a pinch of @sea salt{1/2%tsp}. Bake for 45–60 minutes or until a knife slides easily into the center.

III. Once cooled slightly, slice each potato in half lengthwise. Carefully scoop out the flesh, leaving a 1/4-inch wall to maintain structural integrity. **Technique Note:** Reserve the scooped potato flesh for another use (e.g., mashed potatoes) to ensure zero waste.

IV. Brush the inside of the hollowed skins with @extra-virgin olive oil{2%tbsp} and dust with @smoked paprika{1/2%tsp}. Return them to the oven for 5–8 minutes to crisp the interiors.

V. In a mixing bowl, combine the @cooked chicken breast, shredded{2%cups} with the @clean barbecue sauce{1/2%cup} and @red onion, finely diced{2%tbsp}. Stir until the chicken is evenly glazed.

VI. Spoon the chicken mixture generously into each potato shell. Place them under the broiler for 2–3 minutes until the sauce is bubbling and slightly caramelized.

VII. Remove from heat and garnish each skin with a dollop of @plain Greek yogurt{1/4%cup} and a sprinkle of @green onions, sliced{2%tbsp}. Finish with @black pepper{1/4%tsp}.

# 💡 Chef's Additions & Troubleshooting

* **The Maillard Reaction:** To achieve a professional-grade crunch, ensure the potato skins are completely dry before adding olive oil. Any surface moisture will create steam, which inhibits the Maillard reaction (the chemical reaction between amino acids and reducing sugars that gives browned food its distinctive flavor).
* **Moisture Management:** If the barbecue sauce is too thin, it may soak into the potato wall, making the skin soggy. To prevent this, simmer the sauce in a small pan for 5 minutes to reduce the water content before mixing it with the chicken.
* **Texture Variation:** For an added layer of complexity, fold 1 tablespoon of toasted sesame seeds into the chicken mixture for a subtle nutty aroma and enhanced omega-3 profile.
