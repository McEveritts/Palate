# 🌿 Miso Master Pesto

An elevated, umami-rich interpretation of the classic Genovese pesto. By integrating fermented white miso, we introduce a savory depth that complements the brightness of fresh basil and the richness of toasted pine nuts.

## 🛒 Ingredients

* @fresh basil leaves{2%cups, packed}
* @white miso paste{2%tbsp}
* @pine nuts{1/3%cup}
* @extra virgin olive oil{1/2%cup}
* @fresh lemon juice{1%tbsp}
* @garlic cloves{2%cloves}
* @sea salt{1/4%tsp}
* @black pepper{1/8%tsp}

## 🍳 Preparation

I. Place @pine nuts{1/3%cup} in a dry skillet over medium heat. Toast for 3–5 minutes, stirring frequently, until golden brown and fragrant. 
**Technique Note:** Remove from heat immediately after coloring to prevent the delicate oils in the nuts from scorching.

II. In a food processor or high-speed blender, combine the toasted @pine nuts{1/3%cup}, @garlic cloves{2%cloves}, and @white miso paste{2%tbsp}. Pulse until a coarse paste forms.

III. Add the @fresh basil leaves{2%cups, packed} and @sea salt{1/4%tsp} and @black pepper{1/8%tsp}. Pulse in short bursts until the leaves are finely chopped.
**Crucial Step:** Do not over-process the basil; excessive blending generates heat from the blades, which can oxidize the chlorophyll and turn the pesto a dull brown.

IV. While the processor is running on low, slowly drizzle in the @extra virgin olive oil{1/2%cup} in a steady stream. This creates a stable emulsion for a creamy texture.

V. Stir in the @fresh lemon juice{1%tbsp} by hand or with one final brief pulse to brighten the flavor profile.

## 💡 Chef's Additions & Troubleshooting

* **The Umami Balance:** White miso provides a salt-forward profile. If the pesto tastes overly salty, increase the @fresh lemon juice{1%tbsp} or add a teaspoon of maple syrup to balance the salinity with a hint of sweetness.
* **Preserving Vibrancy:** To prevent oxidation (browning), store the pesto in a glass jar and pour a thin layer of @extra virgin olive oil{1/2%cup} over the surface to create an airtight seal, blocking oxygen from reaching the basil.
* **The Science of Emulsification:** If the pesto separates, it is likely due to adding the oil too quickly. To fix this, whisk in a teaspoon of warm water or additional lemon juice to help re-bind the fats and solids.
